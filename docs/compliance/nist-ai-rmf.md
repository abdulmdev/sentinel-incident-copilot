# NIST AI RMF: where Sentinel Guard helps

The [NIST AI Risk Management Framework 1.0](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.100-1) (NIST AI 100-1) is voluntary. Its subcategories describe outcomes an organization should reach, not features a tool must have. This table shows, for those closest to agents acting in production, what Sentinel Guard contributes. The statements are quoted from the AI RMF Core ([NIST AIRC](https://airc.nist.gov/AI_RMF_Knowledge_Base/AI_RMF/Core_And_Profiles/5-sec-core), Tables 1 to 4).

NIST says the AI RMF 1.0 is being revised; no revised version had been published by 24 October 2026. When it is, this mapping will follow it.

| Subcategory | Statement | Sentinel Guard |
|---|---|---|
| **GOVERN 1.4** | "The risk management process and its outcomes are established through transparent policies, procedures, and other controls based on organizational risk priorities." | Policy as code: per-action risk tier, autonomy and hard limits, reviewed in Git. The version in force is recorded with every decision |
| **GOVERN 1.5** | "Ongoing monitoring and periodic review of the risk management process and its outcomes are planned and organizational roles and responsibilities clearly defined, including determining the frequency of periodic review." | Continuous self-verification (every 5 minutes by default) and guardian alerts. Periodic review uses the evidence bundle's oversight summary |
| **GOVERN 2.1** | "Roles and responsibilities and lines of communication related to mapping, measuring, and managing AI risks are documented and are clear to individuals and teams throughout the organization." | Roles are explicit and enforced: agents (who are held), approvers (RBAC), delegates (services that attest a person's decision), auditors (who may export evidence) |
| **GOVERN 4.3** | "Organizational practices are in place to enable AI testing, identification of incidents, and information sharing." | SafeActionBench and the guard's e2e suite for testing. Blocked-burst and drift alerts identify incidents. The verifiable evidence bundle is shareable with third parties |
| **MAP 3.5** | "Processes for human oversight are defined, assessed, and documented in accordance with organizational policies from the govern function." | The approval gate is the oversight process, defined in the policy. The bundle documents how it was exercised: who decided, how fast, what expired undecided |
| **MEASURE 2.4** | "The functionality and behavior of the AI system and its components – as identified in the map function – are monitored when in production." | `sentinel_guard_agent_outcomes_total` per agent and action, open requests, approval backlog |
| **MEASURE 2.6** | "The AI system is evaluated regularly for safety risks – as identified in the map function. The AI system to be deployed is demonstrated to be safe, its residual negative risk does not exceed the risk tolerance, and it can fail safely, particularly if made to operate beyond its knowledge limits. Safety metrics reflect system reliability and robustness, real-time monitoring, and response times for AI system failures." | Fails safe: if the guard is down, agents are refused while people are unaffected (e2e-verified). SafeActionBench measures harmful actions with and without a guard |
| **MEASURE 2.7** | "AI system security and resilience – as identified in the map function – are evaluated and documented." | Capabilities are single-use, bound to the exact change and signed (forgery and replay tests). Replicas and fail-closed behaviour are tested under load ([chapter 17 §8](../handbook/17-lld-sentinel-guard.md#8-measured)) |
| **MEASURE 2.8** | "Risks associated with transparency and accountability – as identified in the map function – are examined and documented." | Every decision is attributable to an authenticated person or a named policy version, in a record anyone can verify offline |
| **MEASURE 3.1** | "Approaches, personnel, and documentation are in place to regularly identify and track existing, unanticipated, and emergent AI risks based on factors such as intended and actual performance in deployed contexts." | Drift alerts compare each agent's last hour with its own 7-day baseline: rejection rate, need for approval, overrides by people |
| **MEASURE 4.3** | "Measurable performance improvements or declines based on consultations with relevant AI actors, including affected communities, and field data about context-relevant risks and trustworthiness characteristics are identified and documented." | Field data per agent: outcomes, decision times and denial notes from reviewers, in the metrics and the bundle |
| **MANAGE 1.3** | "Responses to the AI risks deemed high priority, as identified by the map function, are developed, planned, and documented. Risk response options can include mitigating, transferring, avoiding, or accepting." | Policy encodes the response per action: avoid (`deny`), mitigate (`approve`, preconditions, limits), accept (`observe`, recorded) |
| **MANAGE 2.3** | "Procedures are followed to respond to and recover from a previously unknown risk when it is identified." | Tighten the policy (new limits, `deny`) without touching the agent. The guard enforces and records the new version at once |
| **MANAGE 2.4** | "Mechanisms are in place and applied, and responsibilities are assigned and understood, to supersede, disengage, or deactivate AI systems that demonstrate performance or outcomes inconsistent with intended use." | Deny per request, per action (`deny`), or for every agent at once (scale the guard to zero: fail closed). Approvers are the assigned responsibility |
| **MANAGE 4.1** | "Post-deployment AI system monitoring plans are implemented, including mechanisms for capturing and evaluating input from users and other relevant AI actors, appeal and override, decommissioning, incident response, recovery, and change management." | Override (deny) with a note; the record of who overrode what; guardian alerts; policy versions as change management |
| **MANAGE 4.3** | "Incidents and errors are communicated to relevant AI actors, including affected communities. Processes for tracking, responding to, and recovering from incidents and errors are followed and documented." | The evidence bundle is the record to communicate. Archived bundles show it wasn't rewritten afterwards. Sentinel's incident copilot is itself such a process for production incidents |

**Profiles.** NIST publishes a Generative AI profile, [NIST AI 600-1](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.AI.600-1). It is working on a profile for [trustworthy AI in critical infrastructure](https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/concept-note-ai-rmf-profile-trustworthy-ai-critical-infrastructure) (concept note, April 2026). We found no profile specific to agentic AI as of October 2026.
